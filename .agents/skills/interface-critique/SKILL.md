# Visual/interface direction critique

## When to use

Use when a visual direction needs an independent review before revision or implementation. Evaluate the supplied direction and available evidence; do not redesign it or modify it unless requested.

## Inputs

- Current brief and product/UX analysis, including goal, audience, journey, hierarchy, and constraints.
- The visual direction being reviewed and any relevant prior decisions.
- Referenced sites or standards when their current content is material to judging the research.

## Process

1. **Rebuild the success criteria from source material.** Identify what the target user must recognize, understand, trust, and do. Do not assume the visual direction's stated rationale is correct.
2. **Test the translation.** Check whether the visual principle, first impression, section prominence, evidence treatment, and action hierarchy actually serve the product goal. Challenge generic aesthetics, unsupported signals of credibility, and visual weight that contradicts the maturity of an offer or idea.
3. **Inspect the system.** Evaluate typography, color, layout, spacing, interaction, motion, accessibility, and responsive behavior across narrow phones, larger phones, tablets, short laptops, wide screens, and text resizing. Look for conflicts between stated principles and concrete decisions. Distinguish document reasoning from rendered observations; when available, prefer another relevant evidence source, such as the rendered candidate or factual source material, over repeating the design rationale. State which conditions were actually inspected.
4. **Separate reasons from numbers.** Flag exact fonts, colors, dimensions, or breakpoints when their precision has no demonstrated benefit. Also flag language so vague that a designer could produce several incompatible results.
5. **Audit research.** Check whether cited examples are current and relevant when that matters. Distinguish evidence about copy or information architecture from evidence about visual mechanics. Note when a reference depends on assets, proof, scale, or content unavailable to this product.
6. **Prioritize findings.** Report zero to five consequential findings; zero is a valid result. For each, state the issue, why it affects users or the product goal, a high/medium/low severity, and a direction for improvement without supplying a replacement design. Preserve strong decisions explicitly.

## Expected output

Write a concise review at the requested location: overall assessment, zero to five prioritized findings with severity, impact, and improvement direction, plus decisions worth preserving. Cover relevant dimensions without creating one issue per checklist item or restating the visual document.

## Quality checks

- The critique is grounded in the brief and UX analysis, not taste alone, and examines the decisions with the greatest effect on the user journey.
- Severity reflects likely impact and uncertainty; minor preferences do not outrank failures of hierarchy, credibility, or device support.
- Each finding gives a reason and a usable improvement direction without redesigning the artifact.
- Strengths are specific and supported. Reference claims distinguish observed behavior from inference.
- The final priorities are few, consequential, and consistent with the findings.
