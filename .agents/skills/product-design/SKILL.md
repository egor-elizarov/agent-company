# Product design analysis

## When to use

Use this skill when a brief needs a product and UX direction before visual design, copy production, or implementation. It applies to new analyses and revisions after a brief changes. Do not turn the analysis into a visual style guide or technical plan unless the user asks.

## Inputs

- The current brief, its goals, audience, constraints, and success criteria.
- Existing product analysis and relevant repository instructions, if present.
- Confirmed user decisions or corrections relevant to this analysis.

## Process

1. **Define the decision to support.** State the product objective, primary users, their likely context, and the action or understanding that would make the experience successful. Separate what the brief says from reasonable inference.
2. **Reason from user needs.** List the questions users must answer and the tasks they must complete. Identify the primary journey from arrival through evaluation to the next action. Account for useful shortcuts or alternate paths only when they affect the design.
3. **Prioritize information and actions.** Rank what users need first, what establishes fit or trust, and what enables the next step. Give the primary action a clear place in the journey. Do not treat every item in the brief as equally prominent.
4. **Propose information architecture.** Group related content, choose a simple reading or navigation order, and explain why it supports the journey. Treat lists of possible features, services, or topics as hypotheses until confirmed; do not mechanically turn each item into a section or card.
5. **Make supported UX decisions.** Choose a direction when the brief gives enough evidence. Explain consequential choices, including what receives emphasis and how distinct or overlapping offers relate. Preserve uncertainty in the wording and prominence of emerging areas.
6. **Triage missing information.** Ask whether each gap would change the next design step. Identify genuine blockers separately from details that can be confirmed before launch. Use a stated, reversible assumption when the evidence supports one; do not keep a long speculative question list.

## Expected output

Produce a concise product analysis in the user-requested location. Cover the objective and target users, primary journey, prioritized information and actions, proposed information architecture, key UX decisions with reasons, and only unresolved questions that materially affect the next decision. When revising, update conclusions that changed rather than preserving stale questions or duplicating earlier analysis. Complete the handoff when the next design decision is supported: identify the resulting direction, supporting artifact, and any material blocker or decision still needed. Do not add an approval gate for work already authorized.

## Quality checks

- The journey, hierarchy, and architecture help a user reach the product's stated goal; they do not merely restate the brief.
- Established facts and developing ideas have appropriately different wording and prominence.
- Every unresolved question could materially change the next step; routine details have a clear later checkpoint.
- Decisions are concrete enough to guide the next product/UX step, while avoiding premature visual style, page components, or implementation technology.
- The output respects the requested scope and has been checked against the latest brief and instructions.
